The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA): What the Labor Department Says

The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) is a federal law that gives eligible workers job-protected time off for certain family and medical reasons. The U.S. Department of Labor explains the basics in its Fact Sheet #28 (revised March 2025).

Who can use it

The Labor Department says an employee is eligible after working for a covered employer for at least 12 months, with at least 1,250 hours of service in the 12 months before leave starts, at a site where the employer has 50 or more employees within 75 miles. Covered employers include private employers with 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks, public agencies, and schools.

What reasons count

  • The birth of a child, or a child placed for adoption or foster care.
  • Caring for a child, spouse, or parent with a serious health condition.
  • The employee's own serious health condition that makes them unable to work.
  • Certain reasons tied to a family member's military service.

How much leave

Eligible employees can take up to 12 workweeks in a 12-month period for most reasons, and up to 26 workweeks for military caregiver leave. Leave can be taken all at once, or in smaller blocks or a reduced schedule when medically necessary. Bonding with a new child in smaller blocks needs the employer's agreement.

Pay and health coverage

FMLA leave is unpaid by law, though employees may use paid leave from the employer at the same time, and an employer may require it. The employer must keep group health coverage going on the same terms as if the employee had not taken leave.

Asking for leave

You do not have to say "FMLA," but you do need to give enough information for the employer to know the leave may be covered, as soon as is practical. An employer may ask for information from a health care provider and must allow 15 calendar days to provide it.

Returning to work and complaints

Employees are generally restored to the same or a virtually identical job. The Labor Department says employers cannot punish or fire someone for requesting or using FMLA leave. If you think your rights were violated, you can file a complaint with the Wage and Hour Division or bring a private lawsuit. Some states have their own leave laws, so check those too.
