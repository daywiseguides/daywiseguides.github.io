FDIC Deposit Insurance: What It Covers (FDIC)

The FDIC's June 2026 consumer news article explains how deposit insurance protects money at insured banks.

What is covered

Deposit insurance applies to products such as checking accounts, savings accounts, and certificates of deposit at FDIC-insured banks. It covers balances dollar-for-dollar, including principal and interest up to the date a bank closes. The FDIC says each depositor is insured at least $250,000 at each insured bank.

What is not covered

The FDIC says it does not apply to stocks, bonds, crypto assets, or money market mutual funds. It also does not protect against theft or fraud losses, though the Electronic Fund Transfer Act may cover unauthorized access to your funds. Review statements and tell your bank promptly about anything unauthorized.

Check your coverage

The FDIC's online tool EDIE (Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator) lets you test whether your deposits are fully insured. Questions can go to 1-877-ASK-FDIC (1-877-275-3342).

Fake bank websites

The FDIC warns that criminals build fake bank sites and may misuse "Member FDIC." Use the FDIC's BankFind to confirm a bank is real and that its web address and phone number match. A bank name used only as a sub-address in a domain is often a sign of a fraudulent site.
