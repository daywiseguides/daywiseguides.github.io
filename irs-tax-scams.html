Tax Scams: Warning Signs and Current Examples (IRS)

The IRS says an email, text, letter, or call that claims to be from the IRS, or a social media post about a big refund, might be a scam or bad tax advice. We checked its page on November 26, 2026.

Signs the IRS lists

A message could be a scam if it is unexpected, rushes or threatens you, offers refunds, credits, or deductions, pressures you for personal or financial information, or tells you to pay now "or else".

Current scams it describes

  • Fake charities: especially after a disaster. Donations to fake charities cannot be deducted, so verify a charity is real before you give.
  • Disaster "helpers": impersonators who call offering help with casualty loss claims. The IRS says it does not call without notifying you by mail.
  • Digital asset compliance letters: fake IRS letters send people to a look-alike site for a portal the IRS says does not exist. Do not respond or scan the QR code.
  • Form 1099-G surprises: if the form shows unemployment pay you never got, you may be a victim of identity theft.
  • Form W-2 credit scams: social media advice to falsify a W-2 to claim credits for a big refund. Scammers charge large fees, and people who try it face penalties and possible prosecution.
  • W-2 business emails: criminals email businesses and payroll companies to steal W-2 data.

If you were scammed

The IRS page links to its steps for reporting fraud, a scam, or identity theft, and for what to do if you were scammed.
