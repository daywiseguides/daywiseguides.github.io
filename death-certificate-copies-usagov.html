Getting a Certified Copy of a Death Certificate (USAGov)

USAGov says many organizations require a certified copy of the death certificate when someone dies, and that how you get copies depends on whether the person died in the U.S. or abroad. Its page was last updated November 17, 2025 when we checked on November 26, 2026.

Death in the U.S.

Contact the vital records office of the state where the death occurred. It can tell you how to order a certified copy online, by mail, or in person, how to get one fast, and the cost of each copy. You need the date and place of death, and the state may ask how you are related or why you want it.

Who can get one

Only certain family members, such as a spouse, siblings, and children, may be able to get a death certificate. Anyone can request one once it becomes public record, and some states release them 25 or more years after death. Check with your state's vital records office.

Certified copy or photocopy

You need to buy a certified copy to notify the Social Security Administration and other government agencies, to close or transfer credit cards and bank accounts, and to claim life insurance or a pension. A photocopy may be enough for tasks like canceling a subscription, so check with each agency or organization.

Death abroad

When a U.S. citizen dies in another country, the U.S. embassy or consulate issues a Consular Report of Death Abroad, which you can use in the U.S. as proof of death. USAGov says you can get up to 20 free certified copies at the time of death, and order more from the Department of State.
