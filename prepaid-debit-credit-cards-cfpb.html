Prepaid, Debit, and Credit Cards: How They Differ (CFPB)

The CFPB says prepaid cards and debit cards are ways to spend money you already have, while credit cards are ways to borrow money. The page shows no update date, so we checked it on November 26, 2026.

Prepaid vs. debit

A prepaid card is not linked to a bank or credit union account. You load money onto the card before you can spend it. A debit card spends money you have in your bank or credit union account.

Generally, with either, you cannot spend more than you loaded or have in the account, and an attempt to do so is denied. The exception is overdraft: some accounts and some prepaid cards allow it. Then you must replace the money and pay an overdraft fee for each transaction that overdraws the account.

Compared with credit cards

All three can show a network logo such as Visa, Mastercard, American Express, or Discover, which can be confusing. But a credit card borrows money that you must repay by the due date. Paying late generally means a late fee, and paying less than the full amount means interest charges. With debit or prepaid, you are using your own money, so unless you overdraw, you owe nothing back and pay no such fees.
