VA Disability Compensation: Who May Be Eligible (VA.gov)

VA.gov says disability compensation provides tax-free monthly payments to veterans with a service-connected condition, meaning an illness or injury caused or made worse by active military service. The page shows no update date, so we checked it on November 26, 2026. Confirm current rules with the VA.

Two things must both be true

  • You have a current illness or injury that affects your mind or body
  • You served on active duty, active duty for training, or inactive duty training

And at least one of these

  • You got sick or injured in service and can link the condition to it
  • You had a condition before service and service made it worse
  • You have a disability related to active-duty service that appeared only after you left

Presumptive conditions

For some conditions the VA presumes service caused them, so you only need to meet the service requirements. VA.gov lists categories: chronic illnesses that appear within a year of discharge, illnesses from contact with toxic substances or hazardous materials, and illnesses linked to time as a prisoner of war. It also notes the PACT Act expanded access for veterans exposed to burn pits and other toxic substances.

Discharge status

An other than honorable, bad conduct, or dishonorable discharge may make you ineligible. VA.gov says you can apply for a discharge upgrade or ask for a Character of Discharge review.

Dependents

With a combined disability rating of at least 30% and a spouse, a child under 18, or a child aged 18 to 23 in school, you may get a higher monthly payment.
