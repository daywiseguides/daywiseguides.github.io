Types of Federal Student Aid (StudentAid.gov)

Federal Student Aid says financial aid is money to help pay for college or career school. Its page describes four main kinds.

The four kinds

  • Grants do not have to be repaid in most cases. Federal examples are Pell Grants, FSEOG, and TEACH Grants. A TEACH Grant can turn into a loan if you do not complete the service obligation.
  • Work-study lets you earn money by working part-time, at least the federal minimum wage. Your award depends on when you apply, your need, and your school's funding.
  • Scholarships come from nonprofit and private groups and may be based on merit, talent, or field of study.
  • Loans must be repaid with interest.

Applying

You apply for federal grants, work-study, and loans with the FAFSA form. The page stresses that the first F stands for "free", so you should not pay to fill it out. Use the form for the school year you want aid for, such as 2027 to 2028. The site also offers a Federal Student Aid Estimator to try before you fill out the FAFSA.

Other sources

The page also lists tax benefits for education, AmeriCorps education awards, and aid from state governments.
