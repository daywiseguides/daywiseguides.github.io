SSDI vs. SSI: The Difference and How to Apply (USAGov)

USAGov explains two Social Security programs for people with disabilities, and for SSI also people 65 or over. Its page was last updated July 16, 2026 when we checked on November 26, 2026.

The difference

SSDI is tied to your work history. It pays you, and certain family members, if you have a disability and worked enough years paying Social Security taxes. SSI does not require work history. It gives money for basics like food, clothing, and housing to people with little to no income who are 65 or older or have a disability. Depending on eligibility, some people receive both ("concurrent" benefits), and the Social Security Administration (SSA) tells you which program or programs you qualify for after you apply.

SSDI

Eligibility depends on your age, disability, and how long you worked. USAGov points to the SSA benefits questionnaire. A spouse, former spouse, or children may also qualify for family benefits. You can apply online, by phone, or in person. If approved, there is a five-month waiting period before benefits start. You can appeal a denial.

SSI

You can apply online, by phone, or in person. To apply for a child, you can start online but must finish in person or by phone. You can appeal a denial.

Payments and help

Your monthly payment day is based on your birthdate. If a payment is more than 3 days late, contact the SSA. A free "my Social Security" account lets you check application status, update your information, set up direct deposit, and get tax documents and a benefits verification letter. The tax documents are for reporting SSDI income. USAGov notes that SSI benefits are not taxable.
