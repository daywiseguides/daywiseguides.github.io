Payday Loan Rollovers: What They Cost You (CFPB)

The CFPB says that renewing or rolling over a payday loan generally means paying a fee to delay repayment. The fee does not reduce what you owe: you still owe the principal plus the new fee. The CFPB page was last reviewed November 25, 2024, so confirm current rules where you live.

The CFPB's example

On a typical payday loan, if you borrow $300, you may owe $345 in 14 days: the $300 plus a $45 finance charge. If you roll it over and pay only the $45, you owe the $300 plus another $45 charge 14 days later. The cost of the original loan has gone from $45 to $90. Roll it over several times, and you may pay several hundred dollars in fees and still owe the amount you borrowed.

Rules vary by state

Some lenders offer rollovers when you cannot pay on time. The CFPB notes that many states limit or ban them.

If you cannot repay

  • Contact your lender and ask for an extended repayment plan
  • If you have a problem with a payday loan, you can submit a complaint to the CFPB online or by calling (855) 411-2372
