What Is an Overdraft, and How Can You Overdraw an Account? (CFPB)

The CFPB says an overdraft happens when you do not have enough money in your account to cover a transaction, but the bank pays it anyway. The page shows no update date, so we checked it on November 26, 2026.

A common trap

Deposits may not be available for use right away. Even right after a deposit, you may still overdraw, so always check your available funds.

How you can overdraw

Through checks, ATM transactions, debit card purchases, automatic bill payments, and electronic or in-person withdrawals.

Overdraft protection

Many banks and credit unions offer programs where they generally pay the transaction and charge a fee, on top of requiring you to repay the overdraft amount. Overdrafts can also be covered by a transfer from a linked account, a credit card, or a line of credit.

Shop around

Fees differ across banks and credit unions. When choosing a checking account, ask about the cost of overdraft protection, a linked account, or a line of credit.
