Medicare Coverage Options: Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage

Medicare.gov says that once you have Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance), you choose how to get your coverage. We checked its page on November 26, 2026.

Choice 1: Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage

Original Medicare is Parts A and B. You pay for services as you get them, Medicare pays part of the cost, and you can see any doctor or hospital in the U.S. that takes Medicare.

Medicare Advantage (Part C) is a plan from a private company. In many cases you can only use doctors in the plan's network. Most plans include drug coverage and extra benefits such as vision, hearing, and dental. You must have both Part A and Part B to join.

Choice 2: Adding coverage to Original Medicare

Part D drug coverage is optional. Medicare.gov says you may pay a late enrollment penalty if you go 63 days or more without creditable drug coverage after you first get Medicare.

Medigap is extra insurance from a private company that helps pay your share of costs. Policies are standardized and lettered (such as Plan G), and the same letter has the same benefits, so price is the difference. Generally they do not cover long-term care, vision, dental, hearing aids, or prescription drugs. If you do not buy within 6 months of first getting Parts A and B, you may be unable to buy one or pay more.

Free counseling

Medicare.gov points to your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) for free, personalized counseling. SHIPs are not connected to any insurance company.
