Finding Unclaimed Money From the Government (USAGov)

USAGov says money counts as unclaimed if a business, financial institution, or government owes you money you did not collect. You may also be able to claim money owed to a deceased relative if you are their legal heir. Its page was last updated September 24, 2025 when we checked on November 26, 2026.

Start with your state

State governments hold most unclaimed money, often from bank accounts, insurance policies, or state agencies. Search your state's unclaimed property office, and check other states where you have lived.

Other databases

USAGov says there is no single place to look, and lists these by type of money:

  • Employers: the Department of Labor database for unpaid wages, and the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation search tool for unclaimed pensions
  • Insurance: the VA database for unclaimed veterans life insurance funds, and the HUD database for FHA mortgage insurance refunds
  • Taxes: the IRS Where's My Refund? tool if you have not received a refund. USAGov notes the period to claim unpaid Economic Impact Payments has ended.
  • Investments: the SEC database for money from enforcement cases
  • Bank and credit union failures: the FDIC database for closed banks, and a separate search for closed credit unions
  • Savings bonds: TreasuryHunt.gov for matured bonds that stopped earning interest
  • Bankruptcies: the U.S. Courts Unclaimed Funds Locator
  • Individual Indian Money accounts: the Interior Department's Whereabouts Unknown search tool
