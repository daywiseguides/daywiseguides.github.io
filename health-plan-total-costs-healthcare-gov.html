Premium, Deductible, Out-of-Pocket Maximum: Your Total Health Costs (HealthCare.gov)

HealthCare.gov says to compare estimated total yearly costs for each Marketplace plan, not just the premium. The page shows no update date, so we checked it on November 26, 2026.

What goes into total costs

  • Monthly premium times 12 months
  • Deductible: what you spend for certain covered services and prescription drugs before the plan pays anything, except free preventive services
  • Copayments and coinsurance: amounts you pay each time you get care, such as $20 for a doctor visit or 30% of hospital charges
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you spend on covered services in a year; after that the insurer pays 100% of covered services

HealthCare.gov's example

Jane's plan starts January 1 with a $1,500 deductible, 20% coinsurance, and a $5,000 out-of-pocket maximum.

  • February: she has not met the deductible, so a $125 office visit costs her $125 and the plan pays $0
  • May: she reaches the deductible. For a $125 visit she pays $25 (20%) and the plan pays $100
  • October: she has paid $5,000 in total, so the plan pays everything covered for the rest of the coverage period, usually through December 31

Comparing plans

Deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance can add a lot, sometimes more than the premium. When you preview plans, you can pick a low, medium, or high level of expected care to see an estimated yearly cost for each plan. At a minimum you pay the premium; the more care you use, the more you pay, up to the out-of-pocket maximum.
