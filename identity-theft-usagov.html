Identity Theft: Warning Signs and How to Report It (USAGov)

USAGov defines identity theft as someone using your personal or financial information without your permission. Its page was last updated July 22, 2025 when we checked on November 26, 2026.

Warning signs

  • Bills for items you did not buy
  • Debt collection calls for accounts you did not open
  • Accounts on your credit report that you did not open
  • Loan application denials
  • Mail that stops coming or goes missing

How to report

  • The FTC at IdentityTheft.gov or 1-877-438-4338
  • The three major credit reporting agencies, asking for fraud alerts and a credit freeze
  • The fraud department at your card issuers, bank, and other places where you have accounts

For tax-related identity theft where the IRS has not notified you, USAGov points to Form 14039, the Identity Theft Affidavit.

Protecting yourself

USAGov advises not answering calls, texts, or messages from people you do not know, not sharing your bank account number, Social Security number, or date of birth, collecting mail daily, reviewing statements for unauthorized charges, and keeping your Social Security card somewhere safe instead of in your wallet.
