Getting a Passport for a Child Under 18 (USAGov)

USAGov says most children must appear in person at a passport acceptance facility, and the cost depends on the child's age. Its page was last updated March 23, 2026.

Under 16

The child must appear in person. A parent, preferably both, must be present and sign the application. These passports are valid for 5 years and cannot be renewed, so an expiring passport means a new in-person application. The State Department's steps cover the form, proof of citizenship and relationship, what to do if both parents or guardians cannot apply, and fees.

Ages 16 to 17

Teens can apply alone with their identification documents. A parent must either give a signed statement that they know the child is applying, or attend the appointment. These passports are valid for 10 years, but one issued before age 16 cannot be renewed.

Other points

  • Expedited service exists, with fees that vary by season
  • Parents worried about a child being taken abroad by the other parent can enroll in the Children's Passport Issuance Alert Program
  • Outside the U.S., contact the nearest embassy or consulate; expedited service is not available
