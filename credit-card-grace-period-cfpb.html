Credit Card Grace Periods: How to Avoid Interest on Purchases (CFPB)

The CFPB says a grace period is the time between the end of a billing cycle and the date your payment is due. The CFPB page was last reviewed November 20, 2026.

How it works

During the grace period you may not be charged interest, as long as you pay your balance in full by the due date. Card companies are not required to give one, but most cards offer it on purchases. If your card has one and you are not carrying a balance, you avoid interest on new purchases by paying in full by the due date.

Losing it

If you do not pay in full by the due date, you are charged interest on the unpaid portion, and also on purchases in the new billing cycle starting on the date of each purchase.

Other points

  • Card companies must mail or deliver bills at least 21 days before the payment is due
  • Grace periods typically apply only to purchases. For a cash advance or a check from your card issuer, you generally pay interest from the date of the transaction

CFPB tip

Pay your bill in full and on time every month. If you pay in full some months and not others, you may lose the grace period for the month you do not pay in full and for the month after.
