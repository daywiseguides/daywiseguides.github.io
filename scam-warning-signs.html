Classic Warning Signs of a Scam (CFPB)

The CFPB says scams can reach you by call, text, mail, email, or online. Its common red flags are requests for money or personal information, pressure to act fast, and someone pretending to be a person or group you trust.

Red flags

  • A caller says they are from the government, a bank, or a business and asks you to send money.
  • You are told to pay money or taxes upfront to get a prize or gift.
  • You are asked to pay by wire transfer, cryptocurrency, a payment app, a prepaid card, or gift cards.
  • Someone wants access to your accounts, cards, wallet, or access codes.
  • You are pushed to act right now, with no time for questions.

Protect yourself

  • Do not share account numbers, passwords, or your Social Security number.
  • Do not click links or scan QR codes from messages you do not trust. Call the real organization at its official number or website to check.
  • Join the National Do Not Call Registry at donotcall.gov or 888-382-1222.
  • Never pay upfront for a prize. Ask for details in writing and read them.

Urgent stories from family

The CFPB notes AI can clone voices and alter images. If someone you know seems to need help, contact them yourself using details you already have, or reach them through another trusted person.

Check fraud

Write checks in permanent black ink, mail them at the post office, and do not leave incoming mail in your mailbox overnight.
