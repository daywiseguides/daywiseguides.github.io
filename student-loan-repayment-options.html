Federal Student Loan Repayment Options (CFPB)

The CFPB says it takes a plan to pay off student loans, and that your best step depends on your situation.

Payment too high or missed payments

The CFPB says to start by applying for an income-driven repayment (IDR) plan, which can lower a monthly payment to as low as $0. It points to the Education Department's Loan Simulator to compare plans. IDR plans need paperwork renewed every year, so set a reminder a month early. If it is still too high, ask your servicer about deferment or forbearance. These pauses are not long-term fixes, and interest can raise your balance and payment.

Default

Most federal student loans go into default after 270 days with no payment. The CFPB says a default can lead to wage and tax refund garnishment and credit problems. The two ways out are rehabilitation (9 months of reasonable payments, which removes the default note from your credit report, and can be done once) and consolidation (faster, but the default stays on your credit report).

Avoid wasting money

The CFPB says not to pay for help with student loans, and not to pay them with credit cards or home equity.

Starting repayment

Pick a plan that fits your goals, make a budget, keep notes on calls with your servicer, and consider consolidating if you have several servicers.
