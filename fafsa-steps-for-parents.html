FAFSA Steps for Parents (StudentAid.gov)

Federal Student Aid says students must complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) form to be eligible for federal grants, work-study, and loans, and that a parent may need to fill out their own sections. We checked its article on November 26, 2026.

The steps it lists

  1. Create an account and gather documents
  2. Start the FAFSA form at StudentAid.gov
  3. Complete the "Student Background" section
  4. Provide consent and approval
  5. Complete the "Parent Demographics" section
  6. Complete the "Parent Financials" section
  7. Invite your spouse as a contributor, if required
  8. Review and sign the form
  9. Remind your child to complete their sections
  10. Check for next steps

The article strongly recommends that the student start first, which saves time and prevents errors. In that case, the parent does steps 1, 4 to 8, and 10.

Tips from the article

  • Every year: a new FAFSA form is needed each year the student is enrolled.
  • Deadlines: schools and states set their own. Some aid is limited, so finishing before the earliest deadline is best.
  • Contributors: a dependent student must provide parent information. A contributor is anyone required to provide information, such as the student, a parent, a parent's spouse, or the student's spouse.
  • Who's My FAFSA Parent? wizard: shows which parent must provide information, takes most people under five minutes, and needs no account. It is especially useful for divorced parents.
  • Accounts: each contributor needs their own StudentAid.gov account. The username and password act as your legal electronic signature.
