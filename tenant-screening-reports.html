What Is a Tenant Screening Report? (CFPB)

When you apply to rent, the CFPB says many landlords use a tenant screening report to decide whether to rent to you and how much to charge for a security deposit.

What can be in it

Credit reports, rental history including evictions and lawsuits, employment verification, criminal history, sex offender registries, the national terrorist watchlist, and a risk score or recommendation based on the landlord's criteria.

If you are denied

Federal law requires the landlord to tell you if a tenant screening or credit report was used to deny you housing. You must also get the reporting company's contact information, notice of your right to dispute the report, and your right to a free copy if you ask within 60 days.

Not the same as a credit report

The CFPB explains a credit report covers credit activity such as loan payment history and account status, while a screening report can include much more. Wrong or outdated information in either can make it harder to find housing. The CFPB keeps a list of consumer reporting companies, including tenant screening companies.
