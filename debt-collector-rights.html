Your Rights When a Debt Collector Calls (CFPB)

The CFPB advises facing a collection call directly instead of ignoring it, because ignoring it can make things worse.

Check who is calling

Get the caller's name, the collection company's name, address and phone number. Ask for the amount owed, who the money is owed to, and how to dispute or verify the debt. If you are not given this, ask for it in writing, and try to have the written notice before you agree to pay or negotiate.

If you know the debt

You can set up a repayment plan. You can also tell the collector to stop contacting you, but the CFPB warns that this does not stop them or the lender from other legal steps, such as a lawsuit or reporting to a credit reporting company. For an old debt, find out the statute of limitations before you pay or agree to a plan. You may want to talk to an attorney.

If you do not know the debt

Write to dispute all or part of it and ask for verification, including the amount and proof it is yours. You can also ask for the original lender's name and address.

Keep records

Keep letters and messages you get and copies of what you send.

What collectors may not do

Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, the CFPB says collectors cannot harass, abuse, or deceive you. Examples: calling before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in general, calling over and over, using obscene language, threatening violence, lying about the amount you owe, falsely claiming to be law enforcement or saying you will be arrested, posting about the debt on your social media, or hiding that they are debt collectors.
