How to Dispute a Credit Report Error (CFPB)

The CFPB says you have the right to dispute errors, and that fixing one generally means contacting both the credit reporting company and the company that supplied the information. If you think the error comes from identity theft, it points to IdentityTheft.gov.

Step 1: Dispute with the credit reporting company

Contact Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion, whichever shows the error. Explain in writing what is wrong and why, and include copies of supporting documents. A mailed letter should include your contact details, any report confirmation number, each error with its account number, a clear explanation, a request to correct or remove it, the marked-up part of your report, and copies (not originals) of documents. The CFPB says certified mail with a return receipt gives you a record.

The company must investigate, pass your dispute to the business that supplied the data, and report results back to you. It can skip a dispute it reasonably finds frivolous, but must tell you why within five business days of deciding.

Step 2: Dispute with the furnisher

Furnishers are companies such as your bank, landlord, or card company. Send a written dispute by certified mail. Furnishers generally must respond within 30 days, and must fix or remove wrong or unverifiable information and tell all the reporting companies.

If the furnisher says it is accurate

You can ask the credit reporting companies to add a statement explaining your dispute to your file. You can also file a complaint with the CFPB.
