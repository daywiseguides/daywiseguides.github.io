Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum: What Health Plan Categories Mean (HealthCare.gov)

HealthCare.gov says Marketplace plans fall into four categories, or "metal levels": Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum. Catastrophic plans are a fifth category, if available in your area, for people under 30 or who qualify for a hardship or affordability exemption. The page shows no update date, so we checked it on November 26, 2026.

What the names mean

They have nothing to do with quality of care. The difference is how the plan and you split costs for covered services. HealthCare.gov's estimates:

  • Bronze: plan pays about 60%, you pay about 40%, high deductible
  • Silver: 70% and 30%, moderate deductible
  • Silver with extra savings: plan pays 73% to 94%, deductible low
  • Gold: 80% and 20%, low deductible
  • Platinum: 90% and 10%, low deductible

Actual costs vary by plan. Extra savings apply only if you enroll in a Silver plan.

Picking one

  • Look at your total health spending, not just the monthly premium
  • If you qualify for extra savings, consider Silver
  • All Bronze and Catastrophic plans work with Health Savings Accounts, and some in other categories do too
  • You find out if you qualify for the premium tax credit when you apply, and it can lower premiums in any category
  • Every category covers the same 10 essential health benefits, including preventive services
  • Compare each plan's 1 to 5 star quality rating
