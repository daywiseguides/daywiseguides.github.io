REAL ID: How to Get One and Whether You Need It (USAGov)

USAGov says the REAL ID Act sets higher security standards for state-issued driver's licenses and ID cards. Its page was last updated April 6, 2026 when we checked on November 26, 2026.

How to get one

When you apply for or renew a license or state ID, you can choose a REAL ID-compliant one. Your state decides the process and documents. In most cases the state licensing agency asks for proof of three things:

  • Identity: for example a U.S. birth certificate, U.S. passport, or Permanent Resident Card
  • Social Security number: for example your Social Security card, Form W-2, or a pay stub
  • Residency in the state: for example a deed, mortgage statement, lease, utility bill, or bank statement

Do you already have one?

If your license or state ID has a star in the upper right-hand corner, it is already REAL ID-compliant.

What it is needed for

Without a REAL ID you cannot use your license or state ID to enter federal government facilities or military installations, or nuclear power plants.

Flying without one

USAGov says you can still fly by showing a passport or another acceptable ID. If you have none, travelers 18 and over can pay the TSA for its ConfirmID program, which USAGov says costs $45 and is valid for 10 days from your departure date, paid in advance. You can also still get a non-REAL ID license or card from your state.
