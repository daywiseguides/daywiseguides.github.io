Home Equity Lines of Credit (HELOCs): How They Work (CFPB)

The CFPB says a home equity line of credit (HELOC) is an "open-end" line of credit that lets you borrow repeatedly against your home equity, which is your home's value minus what you owe on your mortgage. The CFPB page was last reviewed August 28, 2026.

The risk

Only consider a HELOC if you are confident you can keep up with payments. If you fall behind or cannot repay on schedule, you could lose your home.

Draw period

You can generally spend up to your credit limit during the borrowing period, also called the draw period, which could last 10 years. You typically draw with special checks or a credit card. Lenders can charge fees, and some plans require a minimum amount each time you borrow (for example, $300), a minimum outstanding balance, or an initial amount when the line is set up. Many have minimum monthly payments based on your balance. If your home's value drops a lot, or your finances change, the lender may stop you from taking more credit.

Repayment period

After the draw period you can no longer borrow. The lender may set a schedule to repay the balance, often over ten or 20 years, and monthly payments are often much higher. Sometimes the whole amount is due when repayment begins.

Interest

HELOCs usually have a variable rate, so payments can change month to month. Some let you convert part or all of the balance to a fixed rate, which is usually higher than the variable rate but more predictable. The CFPB offers a booklet on how HELOCs work and how to shop around.
