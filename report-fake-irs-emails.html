Reporting a Fake IRS or Treasury Email (IRS)

The IRS says you can help shut down scams and phishing attacks by reporting suspicious emails, texts, social media accounts, calls, and letters. This page covers the email steps only.

What the IRS says about email

The IRS says it never emails without your permission. If you get a suspicious email that claims to be from the IRS or Treasury:

  1. Do not reply, click links, or open attachments.
  2. Send it to phishing@irs.gov with the subject line "IRS" or "Treasury". The IRS prefers that you save the email as a file and attach it, or use "Forward as attachment". Plain forwarding strips data the IRS needs to identify the scammer.
  3. Report it to the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA).
  4. Delete the email.

The IRS also suggests reporting to the Federal Trade Commission. If your money or identity was stolen, report the fake message and follow the IRS's steps for scam victims.

Businesses and W-2 emails

The IRS has separate steps for businesses that got a W-2 phishing email, including whether or not you replied. See the IRS page for the full list.
